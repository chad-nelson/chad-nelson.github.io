Open the dashboard

The source

The data is from the FY 2023 HCRIS public use file on the CMS website.

Data cleaning

  1. Duplicate filings. 63 hospitals filed two cost reports because they changed fiscal year ends.
  2. Partial-year reports. I kept only reports ranging from 350-380 days.
  3. Dirty Data. Occupancy above 100%, average length of stay above 365 days, and zero or negative net patient revenue.

I also edited the reference codes into cleaner labels: facility type from the CCN, ownership structure from the Type of Control field (e.g., Nonprofit, For-Profit, and Government).

The metrics

CMS metric Hospitality equivalent CMS Formula
Occupancy RateOccupancy %Patient Days ÷ Bed Days Available
Revenue per Occupied Bed DayADRNet Patient Revenue ÷ Patient Days
Revenue per Available Bed DayRevPARNet Patient Revenue ÷ Bed Days Available
ALOSLength of stayPatient Days ÷ Discharges
Medicare Day MixSegment mixMedicare Days ÷ Patient Days
Bed Days AvailableAvailable room nightsBeds × days in period

All of the CMS metrics have an equivalent metric used in hopitality. E.g., medicare day mix behaves like hospitality segment mix. Medicare pays a fixed rate regardless of what care actually costs, so a hospital with a high Medicare share has very little pricing power. Just like a property with a high amount of negotiated convention business.

Using weighted vs. simple averages

Simple average of the 5,507 occupancy rates: 54.4%
Total patient days ÷ total bed days available: 65.9%

Every data point in the Looker dashboard uses the weighted average.

Data Limitations

  • The reports are self-reported. CMS publishes what hospitals submit.
  • Occupancy uses licensed beds, not staffed beds. A hospital may be licensed for 300 beds and staff only 220. Real operational occupancy runs higher than this shows.
  • Operating margin excludes non-operating income. Investment returns, grants, parking, and cafeteria revenue are not in it.
  • This is one fiscal year. There are no historical numbers to analyze trends.
