Where the data came from and what it shows.

Open the dashboard

The source

The data is the FY2023 public use file from the CMS Healthcare Provider Cost Reporting Information System (HCRIS). Every hospital that bills Medicare files an annual cost report, and CMS publishes those reports.

What I removed

  1. Duplicate filings. 63 hospitals filed two cost reports because they changed fiscal year ends. I kept the reports covering the longer period.
  2. Partial-year reports. I kept only reports ranging from 350-380 days.
  3. Dirty Data. Occupancy above 100%, average length of stay above 365 days, and zero or negative net patient revenue.

I also edited the reference codes into cleaner labels: facility type from the CCN, ownership structure from the Type of Control field (e.g., Nonprofit, For-Profit, and Government).

The metrics

Every metric in the report has an equivalent in hospitality pricing.

Hospitality metric CMS equivalent CMS Formula
Occupancy %Occupancy RatePatient Days ÷ Bed Days Available
ADRRevenue per Occupied Bed DayNet Patient Revenue ÷ Patient Days
RevPARRevenue per Available Bed DayNet Patient Revenue ÷ Bed Days Available
Length of stayALOSPatient Days ÷ Discharges
Segment mixMedicare Day MixMedicare Days ÷ Patient Days
Available room nightsBed Days AvailableBeds × days in period

Medicare Day Mix behaves like hospitality segment mix. Medicare pays a fixed rate regardless of what care actually costs, so a hospital with a high Medicare share has very little pricing power. Just like a property with negotiated convention business.

Weighted vs. simple averages

Simple average of the 5,507 occupancy rates: 54.4%
Total patient days ÷ total bed days available: 65.9%

Both are correct and they answer different questions. The first says the typical hospital runs 54% full. The second says the hospital system runs 66% full. An 800-bed center and a 12-bed emergency hospital count equally in the first but very unequally in the second.

Every rollup in the report uses the weighted version.

Rural hospitals run 39.2% occupancy with a median margin of −5.4%. Urban hospitals run 64.0% and +0.7%. Across all 5,507, 56% lost money on patient care in 2023.

What's missing or misleading

  • The reports are self-reported. CMS publishes what hospitals submit.
  • Occupancy uses licensed beds, not staffed beds. A hospital may be licensed for 300 beds and staff only 220. Real operational occupancy runs higher than this shows.
  • Operating margin excludes non-operating income. Investment returns, grants, parking, and cafeteria revenue are not in it.
  • This is one fiscal year. There are no historical numbers to analyze trends.
